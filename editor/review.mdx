Preview your changes before they reach your live site, ask teammates to approve them, and publish or merge them from the editor.

## Live preview

Live preview renders your site as you edit, with no build step. With a site page open, click <Icon icon="play" /> **Preview** in the top bar. The preview opens full screen. Click **Refresh preview** to reload it, or **Close preview** to return to the editor. To open the preview in a new browser tab instead, turn on [Open live preview in new tab](/editor/settings#appearance).

To edit site settings and see how they affect your content, click **Site settings** in the preview toolbar, then select a configuration section.

To open the [agent](/editor/agent) beside the preview, click **Ask agent** in the preview toolbar. Only editors and admins can use the agent in live previews.

<Note>
  Live preview isn't available for sites that use standalone [personalization](/create/personalization) without authentication.
</Note>

## Preview deployments

When your feature branch has a pull request, Mintlify builds a preview deployment: a temporary URL where your changes render exactly as they look when published. The preview rebuilds each time the editor commits new changes to the branch. The URL combines your project's subdomain and the branch name: `<subdomain>-<branch-name>.mintlify.site`. Find the exact URL in the pull request comment from the Mintlify bot.

Preview URLs are publicly accessible by default. To restrict access to members of your Mintlify organization, enable preview authentication in the **Previews** section of the [Site](https://app.mintlify.com/settings/project/site) page in your dashboard. See [Restrict access to preview deployments](/deploy/preview-deployments#restrict-access-to-preview-deployments) for more information.

## Which preview to use

| Feature | Live preview | Preview deployment |
|---|---|---|
| **Speed** | Instant | Requires a build |
| **Access** | Inside the editor | Shareable URL |
| **Use when** | Reviewing changes while editing | Sharing with your team for review |
| **Availability** | While the editor is open | As long as the branch exists |

## Share an editor link

To point a teammate at a specific page on a branch, click <Icon icon="ellipsis" /> **More options** in the top-right of the page, then click **Copy link**. Anyone with access to your Mintlify organization can open the link in their own editor session.

## Require review before publishing

<Info>
  To require review before publishing, you must have an [Enterprise plan](https://mintlify.com/pricing?ref=review) and your project's repository must be on GitHub.
</Info>

If an admin turns on [Require review before publishing](/editor/settings#require-review-before-publishing), changes on your deployment branch and on branches you create in the editor need a teammate's approval before an admin or publisher can publish them. Admins can publish their own changes without review.

On a branch, reviewers approve each page in the editor, the same way as on your deployment branch. See [Review changes on a branch](#review-changes-on-a-branch).

Each [role](/dashboard/roles) has different publishing permissions.

### Request a review

1. Make your changes on your deployment branch or on a branch.
1. Click **Request review** in the top bar. This button replaces **Publish** when your changes need review.
1. In the **Request review** section, click **Choose reviewers** and select people or a group. You can only choose members who can approve changes.
1. Click **Request**.

The request includes every page you changed that isn't already in review. Each reviewer gets an email and an item in their [inbox](/dashboard/inbox).

Changes that don't belong to a page, such as navigation, site settings, and deleted files that aren't pages, go into the request as one **Site changes** item. Reviewers approve **Site changes** the same way as a page. Each uploaded file is its own item in the request.

While a page is in an open review request, the sidebar shows **In review** next to it. After a reviewer approves the page, the sidebar shows **Approved**.

Admins can publish without review, but they can also request a review from the **Publish** menu.

### Review and approve changes

1. Open the review request from your inbox or email, or open a page that someone requested you to review.
1. Read the changes. Pages in the review open in diff view, including changes to the page title and description. In source mode, you see a text diff. To leave comments or suggestions instead of approving, use the [comment and suggestion tools](/editor/collaborate). Comments and suggestions don't approve or block a review.
1. Click **Approve** in the banner at the top of the page. Your approval applies to every page in the request.

You can't approve your own review request.

To see a page without highlighted changes, click **Hide changes** in the toolbar. Click **Show changes** to bring them back. Your choice carries over to the other pages in the review.

If a review request contains only site changes and uploaded files, its banner appears in **Site settings** and on each uploaded file instead of on a page. Review links and inbox items for the request open **Site settings**.

### Publish approved changes

When someone approves a review request, the requester gets an inbox item notifying them of the approval. Admins and publishers can publish the approved changes.

1. Open a page in the approved request.
1. Click **Publish** in the top bar. The **Publish** menu lists the requester's changes.
1. Click **Publish** followed by the requester's name, such as **Publish Ronan's changes**, then click **Publish** to confirm.

Only approved items publish. If a reviewer approved the request's **Site changes** item, the requester's navigation and site settings changes publish along with the approved pages.

If another open review request has unapproved changes to your navigation or configuration, those changes stay unpublished until someone approves or withdraws the other request.

### Edit a page after approval

An approval applies only to the version of a page that the reviewer approved. If you edit, rename, or move an approved page, you must request a new review before publishing.

Other approved pages in the same request keep their approval. Only the pages you changed need another review, and **Request review again** in the banner asks the reviewers to review just those pages.

The same applies to **Site changes** and uploaded files. If you change approved site changes or replace an approved file, the approval no longer applies. The banner shows **Request review again** so you can ask the reviewers to approve the new version.

### Withdraw a review request

To withdraw your review request before anyone approves it, open a page in the request and click **Withdraw** in the banner. For a request that contains only site changes and uploaded files, use the banner in **Site settings**.

To remove one reviewer, open the **Publish** menu and click **Withdraw** next to the reviewer's name. If you remove all reviewers, the request closes.

If you discard all changes in a request, the request closes.

### Review changes on a branch

When your project requires review before publishing, branches you create in the editor use the same review flow as your deployment branch. You request reviews and approve changes in the editor, and you don't see the branch's pull request. When you publish, the editor merges the branch into your deployment branch.

A review on a branch covers the whole branch. Every page of the branch shows one banner, such as **This branch is under review by** followed by the reviewers' names, and then **This branch is approved.** Clicking **Approve** in the banner approves the branch in one step.

- A branch publishes all at once. An admin or publisher can publish it only after reviewers approve every change on the branch. Then they click **Publish approved changes**.
- Changes to navigation, site settings, and other files that aren't pages go into the review as **Site changes**. Until a reviewer approves them, only an admin can publish the branch.
- Admins can publish a branch at any time.
- Anyone who can approve changes can approve a branch, except the person who created the branch and the person who requested the review.
- If you move or reorder an approved page, only that page needs another review. Adding a page or changing navigation doesn't affect the approvals on other pages.
- If you edit the branch after approval, or someone pushes a change to it in Git, the banner shows **You edited this branch after it was approved** or **This branch changed in git after it was approved**. Click **Request review again** to ask the reviewers to review it again.
- Pages you delete on the branch are part of the review too. They appear as **Removed**, and reviewers approve them like any other page. A removed page's link in the review banner opens the page on your deployment branch in a new tab.
- If the branch no longer changes a requested page, the page leaves the review request.
- If someone else published a change to the same part of a page, the publish menu shows **Can't publish these changes** and disables **Publish**. Admins can click **View request** to resolve the conflict. Everyone else should ask an admin for help.

On a [sub-branch](/editor/publish#sub-branches), the publish menu still shows **Publish to** your deployment branch and **Merge into** the parent branch.

## Review and merge pull requests

When your branch has a pull request, the publish menu shows its changed files and review status, like **Pull request created**, **Review required**, **Changes requested**, or **Approved**. If Mintlify hosts your repository, a draft shows **Draft in progress**. If your repository is in your own Git provider, such as GitHub, GitLab, or Bitbucket, click **View request** to open the pull request there.

<Tip>
  If the branch has unresolved comment threads, the publish menu shows a warning with the number of open comments in place of **Ready to publish** or **Approved**. You can still merge and publish. The warning reminds you to resolve feedback first.
</Tip>

On a feature branch, reviewers approve your pull request in your Git provider or, on GitHub, from the editor. If your project [requires review before publishing](#require-review-before-publishing), reviewers approve your changes in the editor instead. See [Review changes on a branch](#review-changes-on-a-branch).

1. Click **Publish** in the top bar, then click **Request review**. This marks the pull request ready for review.
1. Reviewers approve the pull request in your Git provider. If your repository is on GitHub and the pull request requires approval, reviewers can also click **Approve** in the **This branch needs a GitHub review** banner at the top of the page. To approve from the editor, a reviewer must [connect their GitHub account](/dashboard/profile#connect-github).
1. Click **Publish**, then click **Merge and publish**. The editor merges the pull request, deploys your changes, and switches you back to your deployment branch.

If Mintlify hosts your repository and approvals aren't required, the publish menu shows **Publish** instead, which merges and deploys in one step.

<Tip>
  Configure branch protection rules in your Git provider to require pull requests. See [About protected branches](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/configuring-branches-and-merges-in-your-repository/managing-protected-branches/about-protected-branches) in the GitHub help or [Protected branches](https://docs.gitlab.com/user/project/repository/branches/protected/) in the GitLab help.
</Tip>

### Compare changes before merging

Click any changed file in the publish menu to open it in diff view against the published version. To leave diff view, click **Viewing changes** in the top bar. Visual mode shows a visual diff and source mode shows a text diff. Files that can't display a diff, such as images or deleted files, appear in the list but aren't clickable.

You can open [hidden pages](/organize/hidden-pages) that appear in the branch diff from the publish menu, even though they don't appear in your site navigation.

### Pull request titles and descriptions

The pull request title comes from your branch title.

If your branch has open comment threads, Mintlify adds a summary of them to the pull request description. See [Collaborate](/editor/collaborate#comments).

## Review an automation run

When an [automation](/automations) opens a pull request, click **View changes** in the Slack notification or notification email to open the editor on the automation's branch. The editor opens in diff view, and the [agent](/editor/agent#continue-an-automation-run) opens alongside it with context on what the automation did.

To open the automation's preview deployment instead, open the run from the **Runs** tab on the [Automations](https://app.mintlify.com/settings/project/automations?tab=runs) page of your dashboard, then click **Preview**.
